## Quant

### Arithmetic & number properties — [Day 1](courses/gre-prep/day-01)
- Prime factorization is the universal tool: **GCF** = lowest shared prime powers, **LCM** = highest power of every prime present, **# of factors** = multiply (exponent+1) for each prime.
- Check divisibility rules instead of dividing by hand.
- Factor vs. multiple: factors are ≤ n and divide into it; multiples are ≥ n and n divides into them.

### Order of operations, percents, ratios, backsolving — [Day 2](courses/gre-prep/day-02)
- A fraction bar groups everything above/below it, like parentheses.
- An exponent applies only to what's directly attached: −2⁴ = −16 ≠ (−2)⁴ = 16.
- Successive percent changes **multiply** (×1.2×0.8), never add/subtract.
- **Backsolving:** for numeric answer choices, start at the middle option — it eliminates two choices per try.
- **Picking numbers:** for variable-heavy percent/ratio problems, plug in an easy concrete number (100 for percents, a common multiple for ratios).

### Exponents & radicals — [Day 3](courses/gre-prep/day-03)
- √(a²) = |a|, **not** a — the single most common radicals error.
- When exponent rules don't directly apply (adding/subtracting exponential terms), factor out the largest common term or look for a difference-of-squares pattern.
- Estimate radicals by bracketing between the nearest perfect squares; compare a radical to an integer by squaring both sides.
- Memorize squares 1-15, cubes 1-5, and √2≈1.414, √3≈1.732, √5≈2.236.

### Algebra — inequalities, absolute value, systems, quadratics — [Day 7](courses/gre-prep/day-07) · [Day 8](courses/gre-prep/day-08)
- Flip the inequality sign only when multiplying/dividing by a **negative**. Flip-proof alternative: rearrange so you add the variable to the other side instead, and you'll only ever divide by a positive.
- **Never** multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a variable of unknown sign.
- Absolute value: |x|<a → one combined range (**and**); |x|>a → two separate ranges (**or**).
- Systems: try adding/subtracting the two equations directly for what's actually asked, before solving for each variable individually.
- Quadratics: sum of roots = −b/a, product = c/a — skip factoring entirely if only the sum/product is asked.
- Factoring x²+bx+c: two numbers multiplying to c, summing to b. For ax²+bx+c (a≠1): the ac-method (multiply a×c, find the pair, split the middle term, factor by grouping).
- Recognize on sight: difference of squares a²−b²=(a−b)(a+b); perfect square trinomials a²±2ab+b²=(a±b)².

### Quantity Comparison — [Day 12](courses/gre-prep/day-12)
- Four fixed choices, always: (A) A greater (B) B greater (C) Equal (D) Cannot be determined.
- Simplify algebraically before plugging in numbers — shared terms often cancel straight to C.
- Test contrasting values (positive integer, negative, zero, fraction between 0 and 1) — if the relationship changes at all across tests, it's D.
- Figures are explicitly **not** drawn to scale — use only the given numbers.

## Verbal

### Text Completion — [Day 4](courses/gre-prep/day-04)
- Predict your own word for the blank *before* looking at the answer choices.
- Signal words set the direction: **contrast** (however, although, despite) vs. **continuation** (moreover, likewise) vs. **cause-effect** (because, thus).
- Don't know the word? Use prefixes/roots to gauge positive/negative charge, eliminate by polarity, and note that GRE-correct answers skew formal over casual.

### Sentence Equivalence — [Day 5](courses/gre-prep/day-05) · [Day 11](courses/gre-prep/day-11)
- Scan the six choices for synonym **pairs** before checking fit.
- Any word with **no synonym partner** can be eliminated immediately, regardless of whether you know its meaning.
- Watch for **dummy pairs** — real synonyms of each other that fit the sentence's grammar but not its actual direction of meaning.

### Reading Comprehension — [Day 10](courses/gre-prep/day-10)
- First read = build a structural map (what each paragraph *does* — claim, evidence, counterpoint, conclusion), not memorize details.
- Track direction words as the passage's own argument outline.
- Match approach to question type: **main idea** (whole passage, not one paragraph) · **detail** (go back to the exact line) · **inference** (strictly textual support, not just plausible) · **function** (what job does this sentence do for the argument) · **vocab-in-context** (re-read the sentence with your answer substituted).
- Wrong-answer trap types: **too extreme**, **half-right**, **out of scope**, **opposite**.

## AWA

### Issue Essay — [Day 8](courses/gre-prep/day-08)
- Intro: paraphrase the claim, state a *qualified* thesis ("mostly true, except when...").
- Body 1 & 2: two **independent** reasons — not one reason restated — each with one specific, concrete example. Use a different domain for each; reusing the same scenario reads as circular even with a "new" claim.
- Body 3: name the strongest counterargument, take it seriously for a sentence, then explain why your position still holds.
- Last 2 minutes: proofread. A single typo in a load-bearing sentence has shown up twice now.
