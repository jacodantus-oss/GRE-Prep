# Day 9: Checkpoint 2 — Second Full Diagnostic

**Date:** August 12, 2026 · **Time budget:** ~2 hours (the test itself)

A full test is the whole day today — no Hour 1/Hour 2 split, no vocab or
drills layered on top. The point is a second real pacing data point before
the Aug 18 PowerPrep dress rehearsal, on **fresh, previously-unused
material** so nothing here erodes the value of your two reserved official
ETS PowerPrep tests.

## Why this exists

One clean data point (July 30) plus one dress rehearsal four days before
the real exam (Aug 18) leaves you calibrating almost entirely on a single
prior test. This adds a midpoint check — far enough into the sprint that
Days 1-8's work should show up, early enough that there's still time to
react to whatever it finds.

## The test

Use **[Kaplan's free full-length GRE practice test](https://www.kaptest.com/gre/free/free-gre-practice-test)**
— a different test-maker than your July 30 baseline (Manhattan Prep), so
you're not pattern-matching one company's specific style. It's genuinely
free but requires creating a free Kaplan account to access it; that's a
one-time signup on their site, not something to route around.

**If that link is down or won't load:** [Princeton Review's free GRE practice test](https://www.princetonreview.com/grad/free-gre-practice-test)
is a solid fallback, also free with account signup.

## Conditions — treat this like the real thing

- **One sitting, real timing, no notes, no calculator beyond what the test
  provides.** A diagnostic taken casually measures nothing.
- **Quiet space, phone away, the breaks the test gives you and no others.**
  Same logic as the exam-day advice from Day 1: use the built-in breaks,
  don't skip them to save time.
- **No looking anything up mid-test** — not a formula, not a word, nothing.
  If you don't know it, that's exactly the information this test exists to
  surface.

**The real wins:** Arithmetic and Algebra — exactly the two areas Days 1-3
and 7-8 were built around — jumped the most of anything on the test.
Short Verbal (+12) tracks the TC/SE work directly; unprompted credit to
"finding the pairs" for the SE gain. AWA +0.5 in one essay. And the
mid-test self-correction on Quant pacing (switching from grinding on
unknowns to skip-and-return after running out of time in section one) is
exactly the adaptive test-taking behavior this whole course is trying to
build — that's not a score, but it matters as much as one.

**Two real regressions, not just "little improvement" — worth naming
plainly:** Geometry and Data Analysis both **dropped** from their baseline
100%. Two honest possible causes, probably both true to some degree: the
baseline's 100% was on a tiny sample (5 geometry, 3 data analysis
questions — not a stable true rate), and this course has given those areas
*zero* dedicated instruction, only occasional light exposure inside
cumulative drills. "Already strong, maintenance-only" was the right call
based on the baseline; it doesn't survive this data point. **Fix:** Day 14
(the review day) now prioritizes Geometry/Data Analysis explicitly, not as
a vague catch-all.

**Reading Comp is a smaller version of the same story.** Day 6's clean
diagnostic showed 8/8 on original passages; Kaplan's real test showed 57%.
Two likely causes: my Day 4-6 passages, even after fixing the domain-bias
issue, were probably still easier than genuine GRE-level RC — I'm not a
specialized item-writer, and it shows. Real fatigue/pressure inside a full
test (you were already tight on time elsewhere) may compound it too.
**Fix:** Day 10's RC strategy content proceeds as originally planned in
full, not the lightened "maintenance" version I'd tentatively suggested
after Checkpoint 1 — that lean doesn't hold up anymore.

**A genuine curriculum gap, found directly from your own note:**
Quantity Comparison — "most questions simply asked me to compare between
two quantities, and didn't have the numbered options required for
backsolving" — **has never been taught in this course.** QC has its own
strategy set (picking numbers across positive/negative/fraction/zero,
simplifying both sides algebraically before comparing, recognizing when
the answer is "cannot be determined") that's meaningfully different from
the multiple-choice strategies covered so far. **Fix:** added to Day 12,
before the full timed mixed set.

**Net:** combined V+Q up 5 points with 9 study days left, real technique
transfer showing up in your own words, and three specific, data-backed
fixes queued rather than guesses. This is what the checkpoint was for.
