# GRE Sprint

**Status:** In progress · **Started:** August 4, 2026 · **Exam:** August 22, 2026

18 study days to close a 9-14 point gap on Verbal+Quant (311 → 320-325) and a
1.0-point gap on AWA (3.5 → 4.5+), for a GRE that only two of ten PhD programs
(UPenn, Harvard) require. Diagnosis from the July 30 Manhattan Prep baseline:
**pacing, not knowledge** — accuracy drops on medium-difficulty questions
while time-per-question stays well under the limit almost everywhere. Geometry
and Data Analysis are already strong (100% each) and get maintenance only;
everything else is sequenced by where the diagnostic showed real gaps.

Full diagnostic detail, targets, and the reasoning behind the sequencing:
[How It Works](/how-it-works). Days 1-3 ran ~1 hour; **Day 4 onward is ~2
hours**, added as practice volume, not new content — see below.

## Prerequisites & positioning

Strong applicant profile (4.0 GPA, active research) — the GRE needs to clear
the bar cleanly, not carry the application. This course is scoped to protect
time for manuscripts and application statements: no filler, no re-teaching
content the diagnostic shows is already known. The 2-hour shift (Day 4+) is
worth watching against that goal — it's real time taken from lab work and
applications, justified only because pacing-under-pressure specifically
needs section-length practice to train, which short drills can't provide.

## Daily structure (Day 4 onward)

- **Hour 1** — same as Days 1-3: review, strategy, topic-isolated practice.
- **Hour 2** — new: daily vocab (~15 min, every day now, not just verbal
  days — vocabulary gains track with exposure reps more directly than
  anything else here), a short Reading Comp set most days (~10-15 min, same
  reps-over-strategy logic), and a **cumulative timed drill** (~35-40 min)
  covering everything learned so far, at real section pace. Geometry and
  Data Analysis (already 100% on the baseline) get maintained through these
  cumulative sets rather than a dedicated day.
