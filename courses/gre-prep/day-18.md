> **Passage.** Before the widespread adoption of standardized time zones
> in the late nineteenth century, most towns set their clocks according
> to local solar noon, meaning that two towns only a few dozen miles
> apart could differ by several minutes. This posed little practical
> difficulty when travel between towns took hours or days, but the
> expansion of railroad networks made the inconsistency genuinely
> disruptive: a train schedule accurate in one town could be minutes off
> in the next, complicating coordination and occasionally contributing
> to collisions between trains operating on conflicting local times.
> Railroad companies, rather than governments, were the first to impose
> standardized time across the regions they served, and only later did
> national governments formally adopt time zones as a matter of law, in
> most cases simply ratifying a system the railroads had already put
> into practice.
